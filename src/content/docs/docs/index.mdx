Stache helps you remember where your things are. Take a photo, put the item in a bin, and scan the bin's QR label when you need it again.

You don't have to name everything or build a perfect catalog. A photo and a place to put it are enough to get started. Add details when they're useful.

## How Stache is organized

- **Items** are the things you own. Each item needs a photo; names, descriptions, quantities, tags, and purchase details are optional.
- **Areas** are the places where things live: rooms, closets, shelves, drawers, and bins. Areas can contain other areas.
- **Labels** are QR codes assigned to areas. Scan one to open that place and see what's inside.
- **Search** helps you find an item by words, barcode, or a similar photo. You can also scan the label on its container.

## Running Stache

Stache runs as one application with an embedded database; you don't need a separate database server. Your inventory stays on the machine where you host it unless you choose to connect an external service. Built-in recognition and photo similarity can run locally. AI suggestions can be reviewed, edited, or ignored.

For operational details, see [Backups and restore](/docs/extras/backups/), [Configuration](/docs/reference/configuration/), and [Security and access](/docs/reference/security/). If something isn't working, start with [Troubleshooting and FAQ](/docs/reference/faq/).

Stache is open source. Browse the [application repository](https://github.com/GraphicHealer/Stache) for releases, issues, and source code.
